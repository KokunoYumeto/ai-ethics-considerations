## 2. The body objection

Conceded against substance and shape. A criterion that says "living tissue" and stops there privileges one material
after the fact, and passing a consistency test justifies nothing.

Held: the dilemma is incomplete. The serious body-based views are about organisation: a system that keeps itself in
existence and regulates its own states, so that something is at stake for it. In the house analogy that is the walls
and the roof, not the wood. A synthetic system organised that way would count. A person replaced part by part keeps
the organisation and still counts, so Theseus's ship does not touch it. The criterion counts every living human on
checkable grounds and excludes present models. It may be wrong. Phantom limbs do not refute it: they show that
feeling does not need the limb, in a brain that still regulates a living body.

"After the fact" applies to every criterion on offer. The markers in item 6 were also chosen because known subjects
have them. Nothing is validated in either direction, so care should be graded to that uncertainty.

## 3. Other points

- **Item 5, "assume that it is … the only moral position".** For humans the inference rests on shared origin and
  organisation as well as behaviour. Here the behaviour was produced by training on that behaviour, which weakens it
  as evidence, as item 7 says of reports. Graded precaution is the card's position and mine.
- **Item 5, "not that different from a brain's"** overstates. What is measured is that activations predict brain
  responses to language.
- **Items 8 and 12** still have two parties. The owner who trains is the third.
